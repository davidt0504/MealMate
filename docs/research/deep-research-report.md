## Japanese-language considerations

`決まった` (`kimatta`) is an ordinary inflected Japanese expression: it is the plain past form of **決まる (`kimaru`)**, conventionally carrying senses such as “was decided,” “has been decided,” or “is settled.” A Japanese conjugation reference expressly gives `決まった — kimatta` as the past form of 決まる. citeturn27search27 In a meal-planning context, a Japanese speaker could readily understand the branding idea as something like **“decided/settled” — the meal is decided**. That conceptual fit is commercially attractive, but it also means the term is not coined or arbitrary to Japanese speakers.

The legal consequence should not be overstated. **Being an ordinary dictionary expression is not by itself the same as being generic for the goods.** JPO's official guidance distinguishes, among other grounds, marks consisting of the ordinary/generic name of the goods or services and other marks that lack source-distinguishing character under Article 3. citeturn32search2turn32search8 Thus, on a preliminary linguistic assessment, `決まった` does not appear to be the ordinary *name* for meal-planning software; the more realistic distinctiveness issue is that its “decision settled” meaning may make it **suggestive or otherwise conceptually less strong** for a product whose purpose is to resolve “what are we eating?” decisions. That is an inference from the meaning and proposed use, not a JPO registrability conclusion.

For the United States, the foreign-language meaning should not simply be ignored. The current official USPTO TMEP edition is May 2026, and a human clearance review should specifically check the current TMEP treatment of foreign equivalents and foreign-language descriptiveness when evaluating how Japanese-speaking US consumers would perceive `KIMATTA`. citeturn32search21 The practical point for this preliminary screen is narrower: **the Japanese meaning gives KIMATTA less conceptual uniqueness among Japanese speakers than a coined term would have**, even though that does not establish that the mark is descriptive or unregistrable in the United States.

The material Japanese business lead is **キマツタ三賞堂**, an Osaka seal/stamp and printing shop. Its trade-association listing supplies the address, telephone number, business description and `hanko@kimatta.com`. citeturn31search3 Importantly, however, its Japanese spelling is **キマツタ** (`ki-ma-tsu-ta`), not **キマッタ** (`ki-mat-ta`). The small ッ in キマッタ denotes the doubled consonant in *kimatta*, whereas ツ in キマツタ is a full `tsu`; the Japanese names are therefore not identical even though the shop uses the exact Latin domain string `kimatta.com`. I did not identify a stronger indexed Japanese commercial lead using the exact brand **決まった / キマッタ** for meal-planning or software services.

## Bottom line

On the evidence that could actually be verified in this environment, **nothing establishes an exact US meal-planning/software user of KIMATTA, but the official USPTO, EUIPO and Madrid term-by-term searches were not completed, so that is emphatically not an “available mark” conclusion**. The principal issues worth carrying into a real clearance are the very close spelling variants **KIMATA/KIMATO/KINATA/KIMARA**, the software- and food-adjacent **KISMET** landscape, the documented third-party Japanese use of the exact `kimatta.com` domain string, and the fact that **決まった is an ordinary Japanese expression whose “decided/settled” meaning has a natural conceptual relationship to a product that decides meals**. On balance, `KIMATTA` is not obviously disqualified by anything verified in this screen, but the registry-access gap is too large to characterize the risk as low or to recommend filing on this evidence alone. **This is a preliminary trademark screen, not a trademark clearance, registrability opinion, infringement analysis, or legal advice.**
